# Hold and Call Transfer Standard Operating Procedure

This SOP governs the rules, permissible durations, courtesy notifications, and warm handover standards required when placing callers on hold or transferring calls to other departments or supervisors.

---

### [HLD-01] Request Permission and State Reason for Hold
- **Description:** Explicitly ask the customer for permission to place them on hold and clearly explain what action you will take while they wait.
- **What Good Looks Like:** The agent states the specific reason for the hold and pauses for the customer's affirmative acknowledgment.
- **Example Phrases:**
  - *"May I place your call on a brief hold for one minute while I check your shipment status with our dispatch team?"*
  - *"To look into your billing transaction, I will need to consult our accounts ledger. May I place you on a brief hold?"*
- **Mandatory:** Yes
- **Critical:** No

---

### [HLD-02] Adhere to Maximum 2-Minute Hold Ceiling
- **Description:** Never leave a customer on hold for more than 2 minutes without coming back on the line to provide a status update.
- **What Good Looks Like:** The agent monitors an on-screen timer and checks back in with the customer before the 120-second mark.
- **Example Phrases:**
  - *"Thank you for your patience. I am still reviewing the carrier logs. May I place you on hold for one more minute?"*
  - *"Thank you for waiting. The supervisor is currently reviewing your ticket. I appreciate your patience while we finalize this."*
- **Mandatory:** Yes
- **Critical:** Yes

---

### [HLD-03] Return From Hold with Gratitude
- **Description:** Thank the customer sincerely for their patience immediately upon returning from the hold.
- **What Good Looks Like:** Greets the caller by name and thanks them warmly before delivering the gathered information.
- **Example Phrases:**
  - *"Thank you so much for holding, Mr. Sharma. I have retrieved your courier manifest details."*
  - *"I appreciate your patience while on hold. Here is what we have confirmed with our warehouse."*
- **Mandatory:** Yes
- **Critical:** No

---

### [HLD-04] Execute Warm Transfer with Full Context
- **Description:** When transferring a call to a supervisor or specialist department, brief the receiving representative thoroughly before bridging the customer.
- **What Good Looks Like:** The agent provides the caller's name, verified Order ID, issue summary, and policy clauses already reviewed so the customer never has to repeat themselves.
- **Example Phrases:**
  - *"Hi Neha (Supervisor), I have Rohan on the line regarding Order ORD-9921 for an Rs 8,000 refund approval. His identity is verified."*
  - *"I am transferring Mr. Verma to you. He is inquiring about a transit delay on an express delivery package."*
- **Mandatory:** Yes
- **Critical:** Yes

---

### [HLD-05] Supervisor Callback Offer When Transfer Unavailable
- **Description:** If a supervisor or department is unavailable within the 2-minute hold, offer a confirmed supervisor callback within 2 to 4 hours.
- **What Good Looks Like:** Apologizes for the delay, logs a callback ticket, confirms the preferred contact number, and commits to the 2-4 hour SLA.
- **Example Phrases:**
  - *"Our senior supervisors are currently assisting other customers. Rather than keeping you on hold, I can schedule a confirmed supervisor callback within the next 2 to 4 hours. Would that work for you?"*
  - *"I have logged callback request #CB-3312 with our priority team, and you will receive a call from a manager within 2 to 4 hours."*
- **Mandatory:** Yes
- **Critical:** No

---

## Common Mistakes & Deviations
1. **Dead Air or Silent Hold:** Placing a customer on mute without informing them or asking permission (`HLD-01`).
2. **Exceeding 2-Minute Hold Window:** Leaving a customer on hold for 3-5+ minutes without checking in (`HLD-02`).
3. **Cold / Blind Transfers:** Dumping a customer into a transfer queue without briefing the receiving agent (`HLD-04`).
4. **Failing to Thank Customer:** Resuming the call abruptly without acknowledging the wait (`HLD-03`).
5. **No Callback Alternative:** Forcing a customer to remain on hold indefinitely when supervisory lines are busy (`HLD-05`).
