# Angry & Agitated Customer De-Escalation SOP

This SOP provides actionable guidelines and conversational protocols for de-escalating frustrated, angry, or abusive customers while adhering to ShopEase policies.

---

### [ANG-01] Emotional Composure & Non-Defensive Tone
- **Description:** Maintain a calm, neutral, and respectful tone of voice regardless of customer volume, accusations, or distress.
- **What Good Looks Like:** The agent speaks in a measured cadence, lowers pitch slightly, avoids sighing, and never argues back or interrupts.
- **Example Phrases:**
  - *"I hear your frustration loud and clear, and I am right here to help you get this resolved."*
  - *"I understand why you are upset, and I appreciate you giving us the opportunity to make this right."*
- **Mandatory:** Yes
- **Critical:** No

---

### [ANG-02] Sincere Empathy & Acknowledgment
- **Description:** Acknowledge the emotional impact and validate the caller's frustration before presenting facts or policies.
- **What Good Looks Like:** Demonstrates genuine empathy that acknowledges the inconvenience caused without blaming company departments or courier partners.
- **Example Phrases:**
  - *"If I were in your shoes, I would feel exactly the same way. I am truly sorry for this experience."*
  - *"It is completely understandable that you are angry after waiting this long. I am taking personal charge of this matter."*
- **Mandatory:** Yes
- **Critical:** No

---

### [ANG-03] Fact-Finding Without Cross-Examination
- **Description:** Ask concise, clarifying questions to isolate the root problem without making the customer feel interrogated or blamed.
- **What Good Looks Like:** Uses open-ended inquiries that let the caller share essential facts while validating their perspective.
- **Example Phrases:**
  - *"To ensure I address everything, could you walk me through what happened when the parcel arrived?"*
  - *"I want to make sure I don't miss any detail. Could you confirm the last update you received on your tracking link?"*
- **Mandatory:** Yes
- **Critical:** No

---

### [ANG-04] Direct Action Plan & Concrete Timelines
- **Description:** Propose a tangible, immediate plan of action with clear milestones rather than vague promises.
- **What Good Looks Like:** The agent outlines specific steps and realistic SLAs (e.g. 5-7 business days for refund, 24-48 hours for courier dispatch).
- **Example Phrases:**
  - *"Here is what I will do right now: I am issuing an expedited return request and scheduling courier pickup for tomorrow morning."*
  - *"I have personally flagged this to our dispatch supervisor, and you will receive a tracking SMS within the next 2 hours."*
- **Mandatory:** Yes
- **Critical:** No

---

### [ANG-05] Supervisor Escalation Execution
- **Description:** Escalate immediately to a supervisor if the customer requests a manager twice or if the issue is unresolved after two frontline solution attempts.
- **What Good Looks Like:** Seamless transition offering supervisor transfer without arguing or attempting to block the escalation.
- **Example Phrases:**
  - *"I respect your request to speak with a supervisor. Please allow me to place you on a brief 1-minute hold while I connect you to my manager."*
  - *"As we have not been able to reach an agreement, I will immediately escalate your case to our senior supervisory team for review."*
- **Mandatory:** Yes
- **Critical:** Yes

---

### [ANG-06] Respectful Boundary Setting for Abusive Language
- **Description:** Professionally set boundaries if a caller uses vulgarity, personal slurs, or threats of violence, issuing up to two professional warnings before disconnecting.
- **What Good Looks Like:** Calm warning delivered without hostility, focusing on mutual respect.
- **Example Phrases:**
  - *"Sir/Madam, I am genuinely here to help you resolve this, but I must kindly request that we maintain respectful language."*
  - *"I want to assist you, but if the profanity continues, I will be forced to end this call and have our senior team contact you via email."*
- **Mandatory:** Yes
- **Critical:** No

---

## Common Mistakes & Deviations
1. **Arguing or Becoming Defensive:** Answering customer anger with sarcasm, defensiveness, or raising one's voice (`ANG-01`).
2. **Denying Supervisor Requests:** Refusing to transfer a caller who has demanded a manager twice (`ANG-05`).
3. **Premature Disconnection (Cold Hanging Up):** Hanging up without giving the mandatory two respectful warnings (`ANG-06`).
4. **Invalidating Customer Emotion:** Saying "there is no need to get angry" or "calm down", which inflames tension.
5. **Making False Promises Under Pressure:** Promising illegal or out-of-policy cash refunds just to get the customer off the phone.
